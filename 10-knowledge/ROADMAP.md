## 3. The hard math

At ~$12 a part and 35-45% margin (assumed until Michael confirms cost), $6,300
of profit needs **1,150-1,500 parts a month, about $14-18k revenue**. Today is
about 170 parts and $2k. That is **7-9x in 8 weeks**.

Three constraints make that very unlikely on the current model:

1. **Cash.** 8x sales needs roughly $8-10k a month of stock bought before it
   sells. With no new cash, stock can only grow as fast as profit or supplier
   credit allows.
2. **Hours.** 1,300 parts as 150+ small orders is far more than 5 hours a week
   of picking, packing and selling.
3. **Ticket size.** At $4-5 profit a part, a shop buying 20 parts a month
   earns us ~$90. Covering $6,300 that way takes ~70 steady accounts.

**What makes it reachable:** fewer, bigger buyers. A repair chain, an online
repair-parts reseller or a regional wholesaler buying 200-500 parts a month
gives the same profit as 10-25 small shops for the same packing time. Their
prepayment can fund the stock. Email outreach still feeds the small-shop base.

**Honest forecast (inferred):** about $2-3k a month of added profit by
mid-December if 2-3 bulk buyers and 20-30 shops convert. The full $6.3k is more
likely by February-March 2027. This turns into a real forecast after 2 weeks of
outreach numbers.

## 4. The sequence

### Top priorities (Jason, 2026-10-10)

1. **iPhone 17 series products ready to sell and iPhone 18 series drafted.**
   New models are where repair demand is growing and where large distributors
   have the thinnest stock, so they are the strongest opener for outreach.
2. **Charging coils (NFC charging flex) listed.** They complete a half
   assembly into a full repair and raise the order value per shop.

The "iPhone 17, 18 and coil listings" thread owns this catalog work. Outreach
emails lead with these products once they are live.

### Week 1: Ready to sell

- Michael answers: is the Jul 28 sheet what shops pay or what he pays; how long
  restocking takes; whether the supplier gives credit or bulk discounts.
- One wholesale page on backglasspros.com: price list, minimum order, shipping,
  warranty paragraph, "apply for wholesale" form. Every email links here.
- Make the GitHub repo private.

### Weeks 1-4: Outreach

- **Shop list:** build a list of US repair shops with public business emails
  and phones, starting with Texas and Oklahoma (where online buyers came from),
  then nationwide. Target 2,000 contacts.
- **Email:** plain-text emails from a real backglasspros address, 50-100 a day,
  with a business address and an opt-out in each email (US CAN-SPAM). No paid
  tools. Follow up twice.
- **Bulk buyers:** identify 30-50 repair chains, parts resellers and regional
  wholesalers; Jason or Michael calls the top 10.
- **DFW:** Michael uses part of his 5 hours on same-day delivery to the best
  local leads the list turns up.

### Weeks 5-8: Keep what pays

- Weekly scorecard: emails sent, replies, wholesale sign-ups, first orders,
  repeat orders, profit by buyer type.
- Price by volume: tier pricing for 100+ part orders to win bulk accounts.
- Stop anything with no orders after 3 weeks.

## 6. Risks

- **Public repo** with the price sheet and Michael's question sheet. Jason
  must switch it to private in GitHub settings; Claude's access cannot.
- **Selling stock we do not have.** Shopify tracks zero stock. Outreach must
  not outrun what Michael can restock.
- **Email reputation.** Too many cold emails from a new address get it marked
  as spam; stay under 100/day and stop at the first complaint.
- **Unverified claims** ("since 2015", "same day before 5pm", "free shipping
  over $150") stay off the site until Michael confirms them.
