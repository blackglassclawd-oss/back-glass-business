## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Back Glass Pros is a US-focused B2B distributor specializing in
Apple iPhone back glass and rear-assembly parts for mobile repair shops.

**What it does:** The storefront helps repair businesses identify and order the
correct model, color, grade, assembly, and charging-coil configuration. The
intended ordering experience emphasizes correct-part confidence, known
delivered cost, current availability, and fast repeat ordering. Shopify remains
the current catalog, checkout, payment, order, refund, and fulfillment
authority while a Cloudflare storefront is evaluated.

**Product category:** Wholesale cell-phone repair parts; specialist iPhone back
glass and rear-assembly distributor.

**Product type:** B2B ecommerce and wholesale distribution.

**Business model:** Product sales to repair businesses. Account pricing, tiers,
minimums, increments, credit terms, discounts, and current shipping economics
are not yet verified in the project evidence and must not be invented.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** US mobile-phone repair shops and repair businesses that
stock iPhone back glass or rear-assembly parts. Company size, annual purchase
volume, and multi-location segmentation remain unverified.

**Decision-makers:** Working hypotheses are repair-shop owners, purchasing or
inventory managers, and lead technicians. Customer research must verify who
selects the supplier, who selects the exact part, and who approves payment.

**Primary use case:** Source the correct iPhone rear part quickly enough to
complete profitable repairs without wrong-part reorders, avoidable shipping
cost, or customer delay.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Identify the exact model, color, grade, assembly, coil, and included parts.
- Know price, availability, delivered cost, and expected fulfillment before
  committing to a repair.
- Reorder known SKUs across repeated repair jobs with minimal entry time.

**Use cases:**

- Browse or search a model-specific catalog.
- Enter quantities across multiple variants in a dense Quick Order workflow.
- Paste a `SKU, quantity` list and hand the verified cart to Shopify checkout.
- Compare verified grade, configuration, fitment, included-component, warranty,
  and RMA evidence.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Repair shops must choose among many visually similar iPhone
rear-part configurations while balancing quality, availability, shipping,
defect exposure, and repair deadlines.

**Why alternatives may fall short:**

- Broad catalogs can make model and configuration selection difficult.
- Grade labels are weak when materials, tolerances, QC, and defect evidence are
  not defined.
- Availability without a synchronization timestamp can mislead buyers.
- Unit price alone hides shipping subsidy, tax, rework, and RMA cost.
- Rebuilding the same multi-line order manually wastes technician or buyer time.

These are category hypotheses; customer interviews and competitor evidence must
confirm which failures buyers actually experience.

**What it costs them:** Potential wrong-part freight, delayed repairs, technician
time, excess inventory, customer callbacks, RMA effort, and lost gross profit.
No dollar estimate is approved yet.

**Emotional tension:** Fear of ordering the wrong configuration, receiving
inconsistent quality, discovering stock is unavailable, or disappointing a
waiting repair customer. This language is inferred, not verbatim customer
research.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- Specialist focus on rear smartphone parts rather than general category
  breadth.
- Working data model for model, generation, color, grade, assembly, coil,
  camera configuration, included components, and compatibility.
- Quick Order, pasted `SKU, quantity`, and planned buy-again workflows for
  recurring B2B buyers.
- Evidence-first product and commercial claims with explicit owners and review
  dates.

**How we do it differently:** Build the buying surface around configuration
accuracy and repeated repair-shop purchasing rather than promotional browsing.

**Why that's better:** It should reduce ordering ambiguity, wrong-part exposure,
and ten-line order completion time. These are intended outcomes; production
analytics have not yet demonstrated them.

**Why customers choose us:** Not yet established by customer interviews,
attribution, or approved testimonials.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Wrong or ambiguous parts, slow multi-SKU ordering, uncertain stock,
unexplained grades, or costly defect resolution at the current supplier.

**Pull:** Specialist catalog clarity, visible configuration and availability,
known delivered cost, evidence-backed quality, and faster repeat ordering.

**Habit:** Existing supplier accounts, saved payment and shipping details,
consolidated purchasing, familiar SKUs, and established credit terms.

**Anxiety:** Product quality, inventory reliability, supplier longevity,
shipping performance, returns, and whether switching fragments purchasing.

All switching forces are hypotheses pending interviews.
